The rule that decided everything

One question, applied to every file in the folder.

Does it run, or does it just ask nicely?

A hook, a gate or a checker survives, because it happens whether or not Claude is paying attention. A document that asks Claude to behave does not, because that is exactly the thing that keeps failing today.

There is hard evidence for this. StyleSeed measured 120 rendered screens: design rules on their own made Claude Code worse, by 3.7 points. The same rules behind a gate that scores the screen and sends it back for revision made it better, by 5.3. Rules without enforcement are not neutral. They are negative.

That is also the diagnosis for the readability problem. The plain-English instructions are 40 lines injected at the start of every session, and they get diluted. The replacement is a built-in Output Style, which changes how Claude writes at the source, plus a hard cap: five rows, three columns, one table. Anything longer becomes a page like this one.

What Codex said when shown the plan

A second model, asked to attack it. It refused to approve it.

Codex · gpt-5.6
The plan correctly diagnoses bloat, but its remedy is too destructive and too enamored with enforcement machinery.

It named three failures worth accepting, and one line it called the weakest in the whole plan.

1. Rarely used is not the same as low value

Some jobs are seasonal. You publish a release when there is something to publish; you ingest documents when documents arrive. Counting how recently you typed something measures recency, not worth. The original plan said seven commands with no outside replacement get deleted anyway. Codex called that the weakest sentence in the document, and it was right: having no replacement is a reason to keep something during a trial, not a reason to bin it.

2. Ten outside tools is not obviously simpler than 37 of your own

Outside tools can go stale, change under you, or be abandoned. A short rule file you can read is repairable; ten packages you cannot are not. The fix is a budget: one design authority, one browser checker, one secret scanner, one claim checker, one build method. Nothing else without removing something first.

3. Two design scorers will fight

StyleSeed and UI Craft both score a screen out of 100. Installing both invites endless revision, where fixing one score breaks the other. Pick one to have the final say. The other, if kept at all, only advises.

Where Codex was told no

It asked for 8 to 12 benchmark tasks, a 30 to 90 day observation window, and formal usability testing. That is a process for a team maintaining a product. This is one person's setup, and that much ceremony is how the folder got to 1,894 lines in the first place.

The revised plan

Archive rather than delete. Familiar names stay. Outside tools arrive one at a time.
